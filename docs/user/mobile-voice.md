# Voice conversations on Android

Open a coding thread and swipe right across the upper part of its conversation, or tap its voice button. Tap **Start voice** and allow microphone access when Android asks. The assistant starts with that coding thread's recent messages, its previous saved voice conversation, project Tasks and any plans you select.

Voice conversations are saved separately from the coding agent's conversation. Reopening the assistant shows its saved messages. Completed utterances save as you talk; a visible warning and retry action appear if the environment is unavailable. Unsaved delivery attempts survive closing the assistant, but cannot survive the app process being killed.

Portfolio access is On by default and your choice is saved for each assistant. During the call, you can ask for documents, code, Portfolio records or other agents' saved conversations from enabled environments. The assistant retrieves those sources when needed; it does not load every repository at startup. Each environment must be connected and support context reads. Older saved history and files can be read in successive chunks.

Ask the assistant to draft a message for the main coding agent. The draft appears in the voice panel, where you can edit, send or discard it without changing your main chat composer. Say **send it** to send that exact draft to the coding thread attached to this assistant, or tap its Send button. Drafting alone does not send. A queued status means the message is waiting for delivery; it does not mean the agent has replied. Portfolio access controls context reads, so turning it Off does not disable drafting or sending to the attached thread.

Mute the microphone or assistant separately, interrupt a reply, or choose speakerphone. Swipe left, go back or tap Stop to end voice. Leaving the app also stops the call and releases its audio. Background spoken completion notifications retain your existing setting and continue after voice ends.

For headphones, choose automatic audio routing rather than forcing speakerphone. On Android 12 or later, allow **Nearby devices** when voice asks, so Bluetooth headphones and their microphone can be used. If permission is denied, voice can still use handset or wired audio and shows a warning. Bluetooth call audio quality depends on the headset; the assistant does not automatically raise your volume or force a media-quality workaround.
